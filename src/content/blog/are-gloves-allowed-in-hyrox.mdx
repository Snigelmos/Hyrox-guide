**Yes, gloves are allowed in Hyrox.** You can wear them on every station, sled pull, farmers carry, wall balls, even rowing if you want. The only restriction is that gloves must not provide a mechanical advantage, sticky tacky-coated palms, integrated hooks, or strap systems that bind your hand to the equipment are not legal.

Even so, **most experienced Hyrox athletes don't wear gloves**, and there's a good reason. Below: what's allowed, what's banned, and what actually works on race day.

## The official rule

The <a href="https://maintain.hyrox.com/rulebooks/HYROX_RulebookSingles_EN.pdf" rel="external">official singles rulebook</a> handles this with three words. Gloves appear on the list of items that may be worn during the race as:

> Gloves [not grips]

Two things follow from how that list is written. The first is the parenthesis: a glove is legal, a grip is not, and the line between them is whether the item bonds or locks your hand to the equipment rather than just covering it. The second is the sentence underneath the list, which says any item not explicitly listed as permitted is prohibited by default. There is no catch-all for "reasonable grip aids". If it is not one of the seven listed items, assume no.

There is also a rule about *when* you wear them that most guides miss. Anything you choose to wear or carry has to be with you from the start of your race to the finish, and it may not be handed to or received from anyone else at any point. Taking a pair from a friend in the Roxzone is outside assistance and can be a disqualification, not a time penalty.

**Standard gym gloves, weightlifting gloves, and cycling-style gloves are all fine.**

## What's allowed

- **Standard weightlifting gloves** (leather or synthetic, padded palm)
- **Cycling gloves** (lightweight, half-finger or full-finger)
- **Gym gloves with built-in wrist support**
- **Thin tactical-style gloves** (think construction gloves with a rubberised palm)
- **Open Hyrox grip gloves** sold by various Hyrox-affiliated brands

## What's not allowed

- **Tacky-coated palms** (designed to bond to handles)
- **Hooks or strap systems** that lock your hand around a rope or bar
- **Gloves with built-in lifting straps**
- **Anything that could be classified as an "exoskeleton" grip device**

If a judge sees something they think gives you mechanical assistance, they can ask you to remove it. They rarely do unless it's blatant.

## Should you actually wear gloves?

Most fast Hyrox finishers race **without gloves** for three reasons:

1. **Rope feel on the sled pull.** Bare hands give you better feedback for a clean hand-over-hand rhythm. Gloves dull that.
2. **Time loss between stations.** Pulling gloves on and off for one station costs 30-60 seconds across a race, and you have to carry them the rest of the way regardless, since you can't hand them to anyone.
3. **Sweat and slippage.** Gloves get wet quickly, and wet gloves grip worse than dry hands do. That used to be an argument for chalk instead. It is a weaker one now that [chalk is restricted to event-supplied pans at two stations](/blog/is-chalk-allowed-in-hyrox/), which is worth reading before you plan your grip around it.

**When gloves do make sense:**

- **First-timers** who haven't built up palm calluses
- **Athletes with sensitive skin** (rope burns from the sled pull are real)
- **Cold-weather races** where venue temperature is below 16°C and bare hands stiffen up
- **People who train with gloves:** race day isn't the time to change habits

## What top athletes wear

Most of the field races bare-handed, and the fast end of it especially so. We can't put a number on that: results portals record times, not equipment, and any percentage you see quoted for glove use at the sharp end is someone's impression of race footage rather than data.

What that leaves is a judgement call, and the single biggest input isn't whether gloves are "fast". It's whether you've trained with them. If your training pulls 5x/week wear gloves, race in gloves. If you've trained bare-handed, race bare-handed.

## What we recommend

For first-time Hyrox racers:

1. **Try a thin-palmed pair in training** for 3-4 weeks before race day.
2. **If your hands are taped or chalked**, skip the gloves entirely.
3. **If you choose to race in gloves**, leave them on for the whole race, don't try to swap.

For repeat racers:

- Race the way you train.
- If grip is your weak link, [a grip-strength block](/training/stations/) does more than gloves ever will.
