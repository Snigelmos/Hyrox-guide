Grip is the limiter that ends more Hyrox races than lungs do, and it is also the area where the rules have tightened most. The short version: gloves and tape are yours to use, chalk belongs to the event, and anything that ties your hand to the equipment is out.

| Grip aid | Legal? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Gloves (not grips)** | ✅ Yes | Every station. Must be worn or carried start to finish |
| **Athletic tape** | ✅ Yes | Every station, no restrictions |
| **Event-supplied chalk** | ⚠️ Restricted | Sled pull and farmers carry only |
| **Your own chalk, in-race** | ❌ No | 2-minute penalty per infringement |
| **Powdered chalk at wall balls** | ❌ No | 2-minute penalty, called out separately |
| **Rosin / grip wax** | ❌ Assume no | Not on the permitted items list |
| **Lifting straps** | ❌ No | Transfers load off the hand |
| **Lifting hooks** | ❌ No | Same |
| **Figure-8 straps** | ❌ No | Same |
| **CrossFit-style grips** | ❌ No | The rulebook says "gloves [not grips]" |
| **Tacky-palm gloves** | ❌ No | Bonds the hand to equipment |
| **Carabiners or clips** | ❌ No | Locks the hand to rope or handle |

The principle behind the bans is consistent: anything that improves friction between your skin and the equipment is fine, and anything that transfers the load off your grip and onto a strap, hook or wrist is not. Grip endurance is part of what these stations measure.

## What the rulebook actually says

Two separate parts of the <a href="https://maintain.hyrox.com/rulebooks/HYROX_RulebookSingles_EN.pdf" rel="external">official singles rulebook</a> govern this, and they work differently.

Equipment is handled by a permitted list. Seven items are named, gloves among them, written as "gloves [not grips]". Underneath, the rulebook states that any item not explicitly listed as permitted is prohibited by default. That is the sentence that decides most grey-area questions, and it decides them against the item. Rosin and grip wax are not on the list.

Chalk is handled separately, in the general race rules rather than the equipment list:

> Only event supplied chalk and chalk related accessories may be used during the race, and only at the designated stations where it is provided. Athletes are not permitted to remove chalk from a station or carry it to another location. Each infringement will result in a 2-minute penalty.

The penalty table narrows "designated stations" to the sled pull and the farmers carry. The wall ball movement standards add their own line: powdered chalk there is a 2-minute penalty.

There is also a timing rule that catches people out. Whatever you choose to wear or carry has to be with you from the start of your race to the finish, and it cannot be handed to or received from anyone. Taking gloves from a friend in the Roxzone is outside assistance, which is a disqualification risk rather than a time penalty.

## Station by station

**SkiErg.** Grip demand is low and the handles are fine dry. Nothing needed, and there is no chalk here anyway.

**Sled push.** Your palms are on the uprights. Chalk would make them worse, not better, and none is provided.

**Sled pull.** The biggest grip-killer in the race, and one of the two stations with a chalk pan. Chalk here, tape if your palms tear on the rope, gloves if you race in them. This is where grip failure costs the most time, so it is worth arriving with a plan.

**Burpee broad jumps.** Hands on the floor, no grip strength involved.

**Rowing.** Three to four minutes on the handle, moderate demand. No chalk is provided and none is permitted, so this is dry hands and whatever you are already wearing. Most athletes find it a non-issue.

**Farmers carry.** The other station with a pan. Sweat plus kettlebell handles is the classic slip, so use it.

**Sandbag lunges.** The bag sits on your shoulders. Tape there if it chafes; hands barely matter.

**Wall balls.** Sweaty hands do cause dropped catches, and chalk is the wrong fix: powdered chalk here is a 2-minute penalty. Dry your hands and work on the catch position instead. Gloves tend to make catches worse rather than better.

## Why straps are banned

Hyrox tests grip endurance deliberately. Straps would let an athlete bypass the grip component of the sled pull and farmers carry entirely, turning both into pure leg and back work. The ban is foundational rather than fussy, and it extends to hooks, figure-8s and anything else that binds wrist to equipment.

Prohibited items are confiscated and returned at the customer service desk after you finish, so the practical outcome of turning up with straps is that you race without them.

## Pre-race grip prep

The window before you enter the corral is the one place your own kit is unrestricted. Chalk your hands there, with powder or liquid, and let them dry properly. Pre-apply tape to known blister spots. Put gloves on before the gun if you are wearing them.

After that, plan on the two chalk pans and dry hands everywhere else. If you want the detail on either half of this, we have full guides to [the chalk rules](/blog/is-chalk-allowed-in-hyrox/) and [whether gloves are worth wearing](/blog/are-gloves-allowed-in-hyrox/).

## What to actually bring

Tape if you have blister spots, gloves if you have trained in them, and a liquid chalk bottle that stays in your bag and never comes out on the race floor.

That is genuinely it. Grip is one of the few limiters that responds fast to direct training, and eight weeks of farmers holds and dead hangs will do more for your sled pull than anything you can buy.
