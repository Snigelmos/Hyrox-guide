Most guides to this question, including an earlier version of this one, tell you that chalk comes in three forms and all of them are legal. That is no longer how Hyrox runs its races, and the gap between the old answer and the current rulebook is worth two minutes on your finish time.

## What the rulebook actually says

The <a href="https://maintain.hyrox.com/rulebooks/HYROX_RulebookSingles_EN.pdf" rel="external">official singles rulebook</a> puts chalk in the general race rules, not the equipment list:

> Only event supplied chalk and chalk related accessories may be used during the race, and only at the designated stations where it is provided. Athletes are not permitted to remove chalk from a station or carry it to another location. Each infringement will result in a 2-minute penalty.

The penalty table is more specific about where "designated stations" means. Chalk use is permitted for the sled pull and farmers carry. Using it anywhere else is a 2-minute penalty, as is using your own chalk or removing event chalk from a station.

The wall ball station gets its own line in the movement standards: a racer who uses powdered chalk there picks up a 2-minute penalty. That is the single most common piece of outdated advice still circulating, because chalk pans at the wall ball rig were a normal sight at earlier events.

## The three rules that catch people out

**Your own chalk is not allowed during the race.** You can chalk up before your wave goes. Once the clock starts, the only chalk you may touch is the chalk in the pans the event puts out. A liquid chalk bottle in your waistband is a 2-minute penalty waiting to happen, and it is the exact setup a lot of race-day packing lists still recommend.

**Chalk stays where you found it.** Dipping at the sled pull and carrying a coating of it to the sandbag lunges is fine, that is just your hands. Taking chalk with you, in a hand, a pocket, or a bottle, is not.

**Powdered chalk at the wall balls is out.** If your hands are slipping on the ball, the answer is a towel and a better catch position, not chalk.

## So what do you do about grip?

The honest answer is that Hyrox has narrowed the equipment route to grip and left you with the training route.

Gloves are the exception worth knowing about. They appear on the permitted items list as "gloves [not grips]", which means an ordinary pair with a leather or synthetic palm is legal for the whole race, at every station. If grip is genuinely your limiter, that is the one aid the rules still give you. We cover the trade-offs in our guide to [whether gloves are allowed in Hyrox](/blog/are-gloves-allowed-in-hyrox/), because wearing them is not automatically faster.

[Hand taping](/blog/is-hand-taping-allowed-in-hyrox/) is the other legal option, and it solves a different problem: it protects skin on the rope rather than adding friction.

Everything else is training. Grip is one of the few limiters in a Hyrox race that responds quickly to direct work. Farmers holds, dead hangs, and heavy carries in the eight weeks before a race will do more for your sled pull than any product will.

## Planning your race around it

Chalk before you enter the corral, using whatever you like. Powdered or liquid both work, and this is the one moment in the day where the choice is yours. Let it dry properly.

From there, expect chalk at two moments: the sled pull, which is where grip failure costs the most time, and the farmers carry. Both have pans. Dip, do the station, move on.

For the stations without chalk, the SkiErg, sled push, burpee broad jumps, row, sandbag lunges and wall balls, plan on dry hands and a towel. Most athletes find the row and SkiErg fine without anything. The wall balls are where sweat actually causes misses, and the fix there is drying your hands, not chalking them.

## Common questions from first-timers

**Can I use liquid chalk before the race?** Yes. Reporting from <a href="https://hybridfitnessmedia.com/2026/06/24/hyrox-competition-standards-2026-27-season/" rel="external">Hyrox's 2026/27 standards clarifications</a> confirms athletes may chalk their hands with powdered or liquid chalk before the race begins. It is in-race use of your own chalk that is prohibited.

**Does this vary by event?** The rulebook is global, but enforcement is not uniform. Some judges will not notice a chalk bottle. Racing on the assumption that yours will not notice is a poor plan when the downside is two minutes.

**What about doubles?** The same chalk rules apply, and chalking your partner counts as outside assistance. Each of you chalks yourself.

**Is buying liquid chalk still worth it?** For training, yes. Grip work is most of the answer to a bad sled pull, and chalk makes heavy carries in the gym meaningfully better. Just leave it in your bag on race day.
